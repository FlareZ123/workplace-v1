## New conditional target-acquisition mechanism
Writing pressure on a page can leave decipherable indentation on the underlying paper. If Emily wrote the missing final margin while a second sheet, pad leaf, or folder backing was underneath, the **underlying original** may contain a physical impression of the lost inscription, independent of an existing photograph or printed transcript.

This is a testable *conditional* mechanism. The email locates notes under a folder on 2 October; it does **not** establish what surface was beneath the final margin, when the margin was written, whether a transfer occurred, or whether any physical original survives.

A [NIST-listed forensic-document indentation standard](https://www.nist.gov/osac/standards-library/ansiasb-standard-044-19) and the [SWGDOC examination standard](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2016/10/26/swgdoc_standard_for_indentation_examinations.pdf) describe optical oblique-light and electrostatic detection (EDD/ESDA) of primary and secondary writing impressions. The guidance warns that prior handling, wet/damaged paper, chemical processing, and repeated examinations can impair results.
