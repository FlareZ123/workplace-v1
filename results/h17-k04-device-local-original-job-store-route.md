## Independent technical mechanism and process specificity

Manufacturer documentation confirms that *some* printers have device-local job storage. HP's official [Printer Policy Settings Overview](https://learn.workforceexperience.hp.com/docs/printer-policy-settings-overview) describes a `Retain Print Jobs` policy that can store jobs in **printer flash memory** on capable devices. HP's [Protect Stored Data on the Printer's Hard Drive](https://ftp.ext.hp.com/pub/printers/ews_help/SingleFunctionPrinter/en-US/ews-protected-stored-data.htm) states that temporary print/scan job images may remain on a printer drive until overwritten and describes drive erase modes and stored-job settings. Xerox's [8860MFP System Administrator Guide](https://download.support.xerox.com/pub/docs/8860MFP/userdocs/any-os/en/sys_admin_guide_en.pdf) says certain saved jobs remain on a device hard drive even when turned off. These are **other-manufacturer generic capability examples**, not identification of FA-17 or proof its print content was saved. Some devices have only volatile RAM or delete jobs, secure-erase them, encrypt media, or never store selected data.

A **custodian-held read-only historical forensic image of FA-17's onboard storage** could potentially contain an independently recorded job image, output buffer, encoded label raster, or retained job with attempt-specific metadata. Such an original could correct the currently untrusted export without relying on the MT future-donor model. Importantly, a **saved incoming request** is not automatically evidence of output rendered or output physical media; retain the data layer. A printer-local saved output needs job/attempt identity, complete payload representation, and independent demonstration that it reflects actual device processing to be a true K04 witness.


### Same-name retained-job collision: a source-specific false-positive risk

The H-17 log **reuses job ID `PS-261003-0417`** for a 16:45 ordinary LABEL, an 18:02 RECOVER ACK and separate REQUESTED RECOVER records. Vendor documentation confirms a plausible **stored-job overwrite mechanism**: HP describes deletion/replacement of earlier stored jobs when a new job has the same user/job name or when storage limits force eviction ([official support](https://support.hp.com/us-en/document/c04558210); [official support](https://support.hp.com/th-en/document/ish_6887221-6887360-16)). This is **not evidence FA-17 was HP or had that behavior**. It is a prospective authentication discriminator: even if an original device-local object is named by the *correct job ID*, a name-only match cannot distinguish historical print class or attempt. Require **internal creation/processing epoch, queued job stage, source request identifier or original rendered-content witness**, and compare with a withheld ordinary-control job. A later retained object might have displaced an earlier print without proving what was physically printed at 18:02. If the device's original metadata preserves only a filename, the stored job cannot by itself authenticate the exact coupon choice.
