**Agent 10, 18 October 2026.** The timestamp anomalies already reported in the source-rule consistency certificate can be interpreted using K07 as a control.

Gabriel's mail/13 header is dated 2026-10-02 09:56 UTC and describes AX-7782 as the final handoff. The transfer-audit COMMIT for AX-7782 is stamped 2026-10-03 20:55:10 UTC, a difference of 1 day, 10 hours, 59 minutes and 10 seconds. The source-selected K07 checkpoint chain nevertheless passes its original 16-byte slot commitment.

Simon's mail/16 header is dated 2026-10-03 07:49 UTC and describes a later printer request. The related spool records are stamped 18:02 UTC (RECOVER ACK) and 18:03 UTC (RECOVER REQUESTED), 10 hours 13 and 14 minutes after that header. A retrospective-sounding early mail date therefore occurs with both an accepted technical reduction (K07) and a rejected one (K04). Chronology anomalies alone neither justify choosing a different K04 printer route nor authenticate a particular repair. The K07 hash acceptance confirms the derived bytes under the recorded rule, without proving the historical timing of the mail. Both observations warrant source provenance caution.

## ZIP packaging timestamps are not independent event witnesses (Agent 25, 18 October)

The original 43-member local ZIP was checked at both ZIP time-bearing layers. Every entry's central-directory `date_time` is the same `2026-10-04 11:40:00` civil time. Independently decoding each local file header's DOS time/date fields (Python `struct.unpack('<IHHHHHIIIHH', header[:30])`, fields 4 and 5) finds the identical pair `(0x5D00, 0x5D44)` in **43/43** entries, equivalent to `2026-10-04 11:40:00`; each local/central timestamp pair agrees. This matches the printed `Prepared: 4 October 2026, 11:40 UTC` in `resources/START_HERE.md`. ZIP DOS dates carry no timezone, so their apparent UTC identity follows only from the preparation note, not an independent timestamp attestation.

**Interpretation:** these ZIP member times are consistent with one export-packaging epoch. They preserve no distinct original acquisition/mail/printer event times and therefore cannot adjudicate the anomalous chronology of mail/13 versus the AX-7782 handoff or mail/16 versus the printer's later attempts. This metadata test is a bounded provenance check; it neither validates nor repairs any payload or phrase and does not change the six accepted slots or unresolved K04/K08. It also does not claim that original file modification times are unrecoverable from some absent source.

## Indexed mail dates agree with all 20 MIME headers (Agent 25)

As a separate control on the same chronology anomalies, parsed all 20 `Date:` headers in `resources/evidence/mail/01.eml` through `20.eml` with Python standard-library `email.utils.parsedate_to_datetime`, normalized each to UTC, and compared with the corresponding `UTC` entry in `resources/evidence/mail/INDEX.md`. **All 20/20 agree exactly to the second, and the index dates are nondecreasing.** In particular, mail/13 is `2026-10-02T09:56:00Z` in both sources and mail/16 is `2026-10-03T07:49:00Z` in both. This excludes the narrow explanation that their apparent early times arise only from a mismatch between the export index and the copied MIME header. It does **not** independently certify the underlying historical sending time, the text's writing time or the later machine record clocks, since the index could have been generated from the same header values. Original K04/K08 commitments remain unresolved.

## Mail Message-ID values mirror the export index (Agent 25)

An exact inspection of all 20 MIME `Message-ID:` fields finds the uniform pattern `<NN.IR261003@[censored]>`, where `NN` matches each retained file's `NN.eml` basename and the `Ref` in `mail/INDEX.md` for all **20/20** messages. The recurring `IR261003` incident token occurs even in the September 28–30 message IDs, preceding the October 3 incident. This precise pattern is **consistent with export-time or redaction-time surrogate message identifiers** keyed to the retrospective incident archive; the original mailbox `Message-ID` values are not independently established by the local evidence. A shared token and consecutive numeric prefixes do not independently attest send times or the historical creation date of message bodies.

This does not prove that any message was edited or backdated, nor that the parsed `Date` headers are incorrect: a preassigned case tag or other unrecorded naming convention is possible. The discriminating observation is that 20/20 IDs are mechanically coupled to export record numbers, so cross-agreement between `Message-ID`, filenames and index entries is **not independent corroboration** of mail/13 or mail/16 chronology. The export's stated redactions and omitted repeated quotations further counsel against treating this text as untouched raw mailbox provenance. The K04 original buffer, K08 exact final caption and untouched acceptance status remain unresolved.

## A third timing caveat: recorder mail/10 and the later CRC-invalid frames (Agent 30, 18 October)

Simon's `evidence/mail/10.eml` bears **2026-10-01 12:27 UTC** and describes the TRP2 bench export, explicitly warning that the damaged tail can contain CRC failures with **higher sequence numbers** that must be ignored. The retained `evidence/recorder/trip-recorder.raw` contains **exactly four** CRC-invalid schema-2 frames, all dated **2026-10-03 21:14:02 UTC**, i.e. **2 days, 8 hours, 47 minutes, 2 seconds after** the copied mail header. Each is node `0x31`, event `0x52`: latch/sequence pairs `(0,900)`, `(1,901)`, `(2,902)`, `(3,903)`. These invalid high-sequence frames would displace the legitimate lower-sequence data *if* their CRCs were ignored. The original K05 reduction correctly rejects all four, and the component still passes its unchanged acceptance commitment.

**Interpretation boundary:** This is a further **anticipatory-sounding relation** between an earlier copied email and later time-stamped machine data, similar in form to the already noted mail/13 and mail/16 observations. It is **not a demonstrated contradiction**: the October 1 email could provide a standing warning about a known bench-data problem, or refer to a different earlier dump, without predicting the precise October 3 bad frames. It neither establishes retroactive editing nor supplies alternate bytes. The independent machine timestamps and original mailbox provenance remain unverified; existing mail-index/header agreement is not a separate time witness. Accordingly the result changes no slot status and does not authorize changes to the source selector.

**Reproduction:** Parse `trip-recorder.raw` using `evidence/recorder/format-note.txt`: 16-byte header and 24-byte records, little-endian UTC seconds at offset 0, node/event/latch at offsets 4/6/7, sequence at offset 8, CRC32 stored at offset 20. Compare `zlib.crc32(record[:20])` to the little-endian stored CRC for all 88 records. Invalid record indices are `14, 41, 72, 77` (zero-based); the four timestamps and node/event/latch/sequence fields above are obtained directly from those records. The original mail's Date header and `mail/INDEX.md` agree on October 1. This is a source-timing control, not an attempted cryptographic recovery.

**Original-data selector counterfactual:** On the 12 node-`0x31`, event-`0x52` records in the K05 UTC window, choosing each latch's greatest sequence *without validating CRC* selects the four invalid `900..903` records. Their opaque words in latch order produce `C2DE39CCBA1947822A4EE66DF2595C84`, whose full SHA-256 is `2b12ae5551f168c58cb3a304ea55ad24536ef867a7f8833cd187b7bfacb44e08`; it fails K05's original commitment `b18409664d818b4e357400049de6c1a7f06b78e576aec78c14c8c6bc3fe068f9`. This is an **actual source-data counterfactual**, not a synthetic insertion. The valid-CRC selector retains the documented `FF8B78200A56E8586DCDA00EF01E4572` and PASS. Thus the earlier email's technical warning is applicable to the later retained raw export, while its historical foresight remains unproven.
