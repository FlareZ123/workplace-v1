## Physical negative-image hypothesis and preacquisition stop gate

Certain **thermal-transfer** print systems wind a used ink ribbon onto a take-up reel. Where ink has transferred to the printed output, the ribbon can retain a negative image of text or graphics. Peer-reviewed print-security work explicitly recognizes used thermal-transfer ribbon as a residual-document source (see [Li et al., 2011, Novel Approach to Thermal Transfer Ribbon Residual Security Problem](https://doi.org/10.2352/ISSN.2169-4451.2011.27.1.art00087_1) and [Conservation Approach to Thermal Printer Ribbon Preservation and Imaging, JASQDE 2025](https://jasqde.org/journal/conservation-approach-to-thermal-printer-ribbon-preservation-and-imaging/)). A printer using *direct thermal* paper, ordinary laser/inkjet methods or a bare feed tray does not offer the same consumed-ribbon image. The original source **does not establish that ESC-N was thermal-transfer**.

**Falsify this hypothesis before seeking a ribbon:** acquire, if already preserved through authorized records custody, the original FA-17 equipment model/consumable specification (e.g. its original `R:/Risk/H17-POL-17/renewal-ledger-2025.xlsx` asset ledger named in the separately supplied office bookmarks, or the actual Stores binder media part numbers), and identify precisely which printing mechanism served the ESC-N incident coupon. Then:

- **Feed-only / direct-thermal / laser / unrelated label printer:** close residual ribbon route. Do not infer ribbon merely from the word cassette, or link crew N22's unrelated mention of a label printer to ESC-N without a job/asset source.
- **Original thermal-transfer or other demonstrably image-retaining expendable medium:** determine independently whether a **used take-up ribbon or residual-imaging medium from that exact FA-17 device and 3 October print interval** survived and was returned in `L-29/N` or another separately inventoried set. Require original part/roll labels, dated service/inventory custody, and an explanation for the post-fire physical survival. A later replacement cartridge cannot certify the incident print.
